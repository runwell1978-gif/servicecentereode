1. The Three Cost Pillars: Inspection, Labor, and Spare Parts

Every legitimate home appliance service invoice consists of three straightforward components:
• Visiting & Diagnostic Fee: Covers the technician's travel and time spent testing the unit with multimeters, pressure gauges, or diagnostic software (typically ₹200–₹350 in Erode).
• Service / Labor Charge: The physical work of disassembling the appliance, descaling, welding, or fitting components.
• Spare Part Cost: The cost of the replacement component itself (capacitor, motor, sensor, or board).

2. Why Fixed Quotes Over Phone Calls Are Usually Misleading

If a service desk tells you "Your AC cooling issue will cost exactly ₹400" over a 30-second phone call, be cautious. A symptom like "AC not cooling" could be a dirty filter (free to wash), a weak capacitor (₹650), a copper flare leak (₹1,800), or a seized compressor (₹4,500).

A reliable service provider will explain the possible causes, confirm the standard doorstep inspection fee, and promise a clear written or WhatsApp estimate after on-site testing before any work begins.

4. The 50% Rule: Deciding Between Repair and Replacement

If your appliance is 8 to 12 years old and the estimated repair cost exceeds 40% to 50% of the price of a brand new unit, replacing it is usually the more sensible financial decision.

However, if the repair involves a minor wear-and-tear item—such as an AC capacitor, a washer drain pump, or a fridge start relay—repairing it extends the appliance life by another 3 to 5 years at a fraction of the replacement cost.

Step 1: Ask if the Visiting Fee Is Adjusted

Step 2: Request the Replaced Old Part Back
