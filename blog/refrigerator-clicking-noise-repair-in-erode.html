Refrigerator Making Clicking or Loud Noise in Erode? Here's What to Check

Sitting in your living room in Eswaran Kovil Street, R.K.V. Road, or Kamaraj High Road and hearing a metallic "click... hummm... click" from your kitchen every three minutes? Refrigerator noises can be distracting and alarming. Here is how to tell normal refrigerator operating sounds from a failing compressor start relay or fan motor.

Quick Summary: What You Need to Know

  • Clicking every 2 to 5 minutes: The compressor start relay or overload protector is tripping because the compressor motor cannot spin up.
  • Loud buzzing or flapping inside the freezer: The evaporator fan blade is physically striking accumulated frost, or its brass bushing has dried out.
  • Rattling or buzzing at the bottom rear: The plastic defrost drain pan has slipped off its mounting brackets, or copper coils are vibrating against the cabinet.
  • Normal operating noises: Soft trickling or gurgling sounds (refrigerant liquid flowing) and a single click when the defrost cycle begins or ends.

1. The Compressor "Click-Hum-Click": PTC Relay & Overload Protector

The compressor is the heart of your refrigerator. To start spinning against high internal gas pressure, it uses a auxiliary start winding powered briefly through a small solid-state ceramic disc called a PTC (Positive Temperature Coefficient) relay.

Over thousands of on-off cycles and voltage fluctuations in Erode homes, this small ceramic disc fractures into pieces. When the thermostat signals the compressor to start, the relay fails to energize the start winding. The compressor hums for 5 seconds, draws high current, and the bimetal overload protector clicks open to save the motor windings from burning.

2. Evaporator Fan Noise Inside the Freezer Compartment

If the noise sounds like playing cards in bicycle spokes or a loud whirring buzz that stops whenever you open the freezer door, the evaporator fan motor is the culprit.

In frost-free fridges, when the defrost system becomes weak, ice builds up on the cooling coils and encroaches on the fan compartment. The spinning fan blades clip the ice. Alternatively, after 5 to 7 years of use, the internal sleeve bearings of the fan motor dry up, creating a dry screeching sound.

3. Vibrating Condenser Coils and Loose Drain Pan

Behind the refrigerator near the floor, a shallow plastic pan collects melted defrost water so the compressor heat can evaporate it. If the pan clips break or the pan vibrates against the copper tubing, it produces an annoying buzzing rattle.

Similarly, if the refrigerator is slightly tilted on uneven kitchen floor tiles in older houses in Eswaran Kovil Street, the compressor rubber mounting grommets transmit vibration directly into the floor.

4. Normal Sounds vs Warning Sounds: A Quick Comparison

• Gurgling or bubbling: Normal refrigerant liquid boiling inside evaporator tubes.
• Soft popping or cracking: Normal plastic cabinet expansion during defrost heating.
• Steady low hum: Normal compressor operation (38 to 45 decibels).
• Repeated loud clicking: Abnormal start relay or compressor motor stall.
• High-pitched metallic screeching: Abnormal fan motor bearing failure.

Practical Step-by-Step Checks for Erode Homes

Before calling for paid service, take 5 minutes to run through these safe, simple checks:

✓

Step 1: Locate the Source of the Sound

Listen carefully: Is the sound coming from inside the top freezer compartment, or down low at the backside of the refrigerator?

✓

Step 2: The Freezer Door Open/Close Test

Open the freezer door while the noise is happening. In most fridges, opening the door cuts power to the evaporator fan. If the sound stops immediately, the fan is the cause.

✓

Step 3: Level the Fridge Feet

Place your hands on top of the fridge and rock it gently. If it wobbles, adjust the front leveling screw feet until all four corners sit solidly on the floor.

✓

Step 4: Check Backside Spacing

Gently pull the fridge forward by 2 inches to ensure the rear metal condenser coils are not vibrating against the wall or electrical cords.

When You Should Stop and Call for Professional Repair

Certain symptoms require immediate attention from a technician with insulated diagnostic tools to prevent permanent damage or safety hazards:

  • The compressor clicks every few minutes and the refrigerator is no longer keeping milk or water cold.
  • The compressor body feels boiling hot to the touch (cannot keep your palm on it for 3 seconds).
  • The freezer fan makes a loud grinding noise that does not stop.
  • A strong burnt plastic or electrical smell emanates from the rear compressor area.
  • The refrigerator clicks off and trips your main household distribution board breaker.
